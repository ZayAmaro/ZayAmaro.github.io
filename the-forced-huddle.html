The Forced Huddle: When AI Becomes a Mandatory Teammate

March 20, 2026

I just read a fascinating piece on Olivia’s Disney Blog titled "Forced Partnerships." She analyzes how companies like Disney are spending billions to bake AI into their "operating system," effectively forcing human creatives to "collaborate" with algorithms whether they want to or not. Olivia quotes Sam Levine, who notes that these tools aren't just brushes—they are coworkers you never asked for. As I looked at her analysis of Disney’s VP of "Human-AI Collaboration," I realized this is exactly what’s happening in the NFL front offices. We are moving toward a "Forced Huddle."

In the past, a scout used a computer to look up stats. Today, the computer is the scout. Olivia’s point about "institutionalized forced partnership" hits hard when you apply it to a locker room. If a General Manager is forced by ownership to use a specific AI-driven draft model, the human scout’s "Taste" (as Jonas Rodrigues called it) is effectively sidelined. The AI isn't a tool the scout uses; the scout is becoming a data-entry clerk for the AI. This is the death of the "Invisible Superstar" I wrote about last time. If the partnership is forced, the algorithm will always favor the data it can see over the leadership it can’t.

The Death of Autonomy

Olivia notes that in Disney’s world, AI is becoming the "rendering engine" of creativity. In sports, AI is becoming the "decision engine" of strategy. When a coach is forced to go for it on 4th down because the "Decision Bot" says there is a 54% success rate, the coach loses his **Moral Agency**. If he succeeds, the bot gets the credit. If he fails, he gets the blame for "not following the data." This forced partnership removes the courage required to lead. It turns the head coach into a middle manager who is just executing a pre-calculated script.

As mentioned in a Harvard Business Review article on the perils of forced collaboration, when humans are forced to work with AI, they often "check out" mentally. They stop looking for the 30% gap—the randomness—because they assume the machine has it covered. This is the most dangerous part for sports. If players and coaches stop trusting their instincts because they are forced to trust the machine, the game loses its soul. We don't want a "Collaboration VP" on the sidelines; we want a leader who has the faith to ignore the machine when the moment demands it.

The Final Whistle on Collaboration

Olivia’s blog shows that even the "Magic" of Disney is being quantified and automated. My blog has argued from day one that the magic of sports is in the human element that *cannot* be automated. A forced partnership isn't a partnership at all—it’s a takeover. Whether it’s Mickey Mouse or a Sunday night kickoff, the moment we stop choosing to lead and start being forced to follow an algorithm, the magic is gone. I’m sticking with the human huddle, messy and unoptimized as it may be.
